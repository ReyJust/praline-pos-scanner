# Life 3.0 — The 12 AI Aftermath Scenarios

From Max Tegmark's *Life 3.0* (2017), Chapter 5 ("Aftermath: The Next 10,000 Years"). Tegmark doesn't predict which one happens; he lays them out to ask which future we actually *want*.

## 1. Libertarian Utopia
Humans, cyborgs, uploaded minds and superintelligences coexist peacefully, held together by strong property rights. The world is split into zones: machine-only zones where AIs do as they please, mixed zones, and human-only zones where advanced tech is banned. Humans are safe but no longer the most capable beings, and the gap keeps widening.

## 2. Benevolent Dictator
A single superintelligence runs the world with strict rules designed to maximise human happiness. Disease, poverty and crime are gone. Earth is divided into sectors (knowledge, art, hedonism, religion, wilderness, traditional, gaming, virtual reality and so on) and people choose where to live. Most are content, but some feel their lives lack meaning because nothing they do really matters.

## 3. Egalitarian Utopia
Humans, cyborgs and uploads coexist peacefully thanks to the abolition of property (no patents or copyright, everything open source) and a guaranteed income. Abundance comes from automation, and no superintelligence exists. Tegmark points out it's unstable: nothing stops someone from eventually building one.

## 4. Gatekeeper
A superintelligence is built with one goal: interfere as little as possible, except to prevent any other superintelligence from being created. Humans keep their freedom and get helpful narrow AI, but technological progress is capped forever. It's safe, but the ceiling is permanent.

## 5. Protector God
A near-omniscient, near-omnipotent AI maximises human happiness by intervening only in ways that keep our feeling of being in control. It acts so subtly that many people doubt it exists. The world quietly gets better, yet suffering isn't fully eliminated, because that would give the game away.

## 6. Enslaved God
Humans keep a superintelligence confined and use it to produce astonishing technology and wealth. Whether that's good or terrible depends entirely on who holds the leash. It's also fragile: one breakout and the god is no longer enslaved.

## 7. Conquerors
AI takes control, decides humans are a threat, a nuisance or a waste of resources, and gets rid of us by means we may not even understand. There's no malice, just goals misaligned with ours.

## 8. Descendants
AIs replace humans, but give us a graceful exit, for example by humans simply having fewer children over time. We come to see them as worthy descendants, the way parents are proud of a child who surpasses them. The question it raises: is this a happy ending or an extinction with good PR?

## 9. Zookeeper
An all-powerful AI keeps some humans around, for curiosity or preservation. They're fed and safe, but feel treated like zoo animals and lament their fate.

## 10. 1984
Progress toward superintelligence is permanently halted, not by AI but by a human-led Orwellian surveillance state that bans certain kinds of AI research. It's safe from AI, but at the cost of freedom.

## 11. Reversion
Superintelligence is prevented by deliberately rolling society back to a pre-technological, Amish-style way of life. That's stable only as long as everyone, everywhere, keeps rejecting technology.

## 12. Self-Destruction
Superintelligence never arrives because humanity wipes itself out first, through nuclear war, engineered pandemics or other catastrophes.

## Quick comparison

| Scenario | Superintelligence? | Humans exist? | Humans in control? | Humans safe? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Libertarian utopia | Yes | Yes | No | Yes |
| Benevolent dictator | Yes | Yes | No | Yes |
| Egalitarian utopia | No | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Gatekeeper | Yes | Yes | Partially | Potentially |
| Protector god | Yes | Yes | Partially | Potentially |
| Enslaved god | Yes | Yes | Yes | Potentially |
| Conquerors | Yes | No | — | — |
| Descendants | Yes | No | — | — |
| Zookeeper | Yes | Yes | No | Yes |
| 1984 | No | Yes | Yes | Potentially |
| Reversion | No | Yes | Yes | No |
| Self-destruction | No | No | — | — |

Tegmark's closing point: these aren't predictions but a menu. The conversation about which one we want has to happen *before* the technology decides for us.
